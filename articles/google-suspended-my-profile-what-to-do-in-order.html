Google Suspended My Profile: What To Do, In Order

Direct answer

A suspended Google Business Profile is almost never random. In nearly every case, something on the profile broke one of Google's rules — the name, the address, a duplicate, or a verification that did not match. The recovery that works always follows the same order: find the cause, fix the cause, and only then appeal — once, with documents that prove the business is real. Google allows one appeal, and a half-ready appeal is a wasted one.

One morning your business is on Google Maps. The next, it is gone. No warning you understood, no reason you can see — and every customer who searches for you finds a competitor instead. If that is where you are right now, take a breath: a suspension feels like a verdict, but it is really a diagnosis waiting to be made.

This is not rare, and it is not just you. Owners have been reporting unexplained suspensions in waves — on Google's own Business Profile page, a single post this month drew dozens of comments from owners whose profiles disappeared without a clear explanation. What follows is the calm version of what to do: the four causes behind almost every suspension, the three mistakes that make recovery harder, and the exact order that gives your one appeal its best chance.

The four causes: check them in this order

Google suspends profiles that break its guidelines for how a business is represented. In practice, almost every suspension traces back to one of these four:

  • The name. Your Google name must be your real-world business name — the name on your sign, your license, and your documents. Adding keywords, a city, or a service to it is one of the most common triggers, because Google treats a decorated name as misrepresentation.
  • The address. A P.O. box is not an acceptable address, and a virtual office is not an eligible location. And if you work at your customers' locations — cleaning, repairs, landscaping — you are a service-area business: your home address should be hidden, not published, and the profile set up the other way.
  • A duplicate. Google allows only one profile per business. An old profile you forgot about, or one a marketing company created for you years ago, can get the real one suspended.
  • Verification. Google asked for proof — a video, a document — and what it received did not match the profile: a different name, a different address, a location that does not look like what the business claims to be.

Read your profile against those four, line by line, before you touch anything else. The cause is almost always visible once you know where to look.

The three mistakes that make it worse

Most owners, in a panic, do one of these three things — and each one lowers the chance of getting the profile back:

  1. Creating a brand-new profile. Now there are two profiles for one business — a duplicate, which is itself a cause of suspension. Google also instructs owners not to create a new Business Profile while an appeal is under review. The new profile usually gets suspended too.
  2. Appealing immediately, with nothing fixed. An appeal that asks for mercy while the violation is still on the profile gives the reviewer nothing to approve. Appeals can only be submitted once; a half-ready appeal spends your one shot.
  3. Paying someone who guarantees reinstatement. Nobody can guarantee it. Google decides, and only Google. A promise of guaranteed recovery is how you recognize the scam — and profiles handled by aggressive fixers sometimes end up in worse shape than the suspension left them.

The recovery, in order

  1. Diagnose. Compare your profile, line by line, against Google's guidelines and the four causes above. Find the specific problem before changing anything — random edits can look like more violations.
  2. Fix the cause. Restore the real business name. Correct the address setup — a real location, or a hidden address with service areas if you work at customers' homes. Remove or merge the duplicate. Get your documents consistent: the same name and address everywhere.
  3. Appeal once, with your evidence ready. Submit the appeal through Google's official process, with the evidence pack below attached. This is the one appeal — make it complete.
  4. Wait — and use the second chance correctly if you need it. A decision can take days, sometimes weeks. If the appeal is denied, you can request one additional review, but only with additional evidence that was not in the original appeal. Sending the same material again is not a second chance; stronger proof is.

The evidence pack

Reviewers approve what they can verify. Before you appeal, gather documents that prove the business is real and operates where the profile says it does:

  • Business registration or business license showing the legal name
  • A utility bill or a lease with the same business name and address as the profile
  • Photos of the location: the storefront, signage with the business name, the interior or workspace
  • For service-area businesses: photos of the branded vehicle, tools and equipment, and invoices or work orders with the business name

Names and addresses must match across every document and the profile itself. A mismatch that looks small to you is exactly what a reviewer is trained to catch.

So it never happens again

When the profile comes back, keep it back. Keep one profile, under the real name. Keep a folder — physical or digital — with the registration, license, and bills that match it, so the next verification is a five-minute task instead of a crisis. Make big changes — name, address, phone — carefully and one at a time, and check the profile after each one. Most suspensions are not attacks; they are accidents that were waiting for a date.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Google suspend my Business Profile?

Almost never at random. In most cases the profile broke one of Google's guidelines: the name is not the real-world business name (keywords, a city, or a service were added), the address is a P.O. box, a virtual office, or a home address that should be hidden for a service-area business, a duplicate profile exists for the same business, or a verification check did not match the profile. An appeal only has a real chance once the cause is fixed.

Can I just create a new profile and start over?

No — that usually makes things worse. A second profile for the same business is a duplicate, which is itself one of the causes of suspension, and Google instructs owners not to create a new Business Profile while an appeal is under review. The new profile typically ends up suspended as well.

How long does a suspension appeal take?

It varies — days, sometimes weeks. An appeal can only be submitted once, so it should go in complete, with evidence, after the cause is fixed. If it is denied, you can request one additional review, but only with additional evidence that was not included in the original appeal.

Can anyone guarantee that my profile will be reinstated?

No. Google decides, and only Google. Anyone who guarantees reinstatement is a red flag. What careful work can promise is process, not outcome: the cause fixed first, and the single appeal submitted complete and in the right order.
