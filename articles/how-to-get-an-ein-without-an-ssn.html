How do I get an EIN without an SSN? With Form SS-4 and a fax

Direct answer

Yes, you can. If you don't have a Social Security number, your LLC's EIN is requested with IRS Form SS-4, sent by fax. It is not a shortcut or a trick: it is the official path for those who cannot use the online application. And it is free: the IRS charges nothing to issue it.

First: what the EIN is, and why you can't skip it

The EIN — Employer Identification Number — is your company's tax number, a nine-digit number the IRS assigns to it. Without it there is no business bank account, no Stripe, and nothing of what comes after forming the LLC. Forming the company and getting the EIN are two different steps, and this is the second one.

Why the IRS website closes the door on you

If you try to request the EIN on the IRS website, the online form will ask you for a Social Security number, and without one it won't let you move forward. That is not the end of the road: it is just a closed door. The online application is made for those who have an SSN or an ITIN and are in the United States. If that is not your case, the IRS has another path, on paper.

The path: Form SS-4 by fax, step by step

The process has four steps, and none of them costs money:

  1. Download the current Form SS-4 from the IRS website (irs.gov/FormSS4). Use the current revision, not an old copy you have saved.
  2. Fill it out with your LLC's information. On line 7b, where it asks for the SSN or ITIN of the responsible party, write the word Foreign — that is what the current IRS instructions say when the responsible party has neither an SSN nor an ITIN and cannot obtain one.
  3. Send it by fax to the number that matches your case. If your LLC was formed in a US state, the number is 855-641-6935. If your LLC has no legal residence or principal place of business in any state, the number changes: 855-215-1627 if you send the fax from inside the United States, or 304-707-9471 if you send it from outside. Also write your own fax number on the form, so the IRS can return the EIN to you by that same route.
  4. Wait for the reply by fax. The IRS returns the EIN by fax when the form includes a return number.

A detail worth its weight in gold: verify the fax number in the SS-4 instructions on the very day you send it. IRS numbers can change without notice, and a fax to the wrong number is time lost.

How long it really takes

The IRS says that through the Fax-TIN program the EIN generally arrives by fax within about four business days. In practice, it sometimes takes weeks. It isn't that something went wrong: it is that the paper process moves at its own pace. Send it early, before you need it to open the bank account, and be patient — it arrives.

The EIN is free — and that needs to be said clearly

The IRS does not charge anything to issue the EIN. If someone charges you to process it, listen carefully: what you are paying for is the service of the person who processes it for you, nothing more. The EIN itself is free, straight from the IRS, at no government cost. Nobody should sell you the number as if it were a product.

And if you do have an SSN or an ITIN?

Then your path is shorter: with an SSN or an ITIN you can request the EIN online and receive it in the same session. And there is a third path worth knowing: international applicants who have no legal residence or place of business in the United States can request the EIN by phone at 267-941-1099, Monday through Friday. Mind you, that line is only for international applicants; if your case is the fax case, the fax is still your path.

Information verified on November 18, 2026 against the current IRS Form SS-4 instructions (December 2025 revision).

The idea that matters

The EIN is free, and the path without an SSN exists: one form and one fax. The door that closes online is not the end of the road.
