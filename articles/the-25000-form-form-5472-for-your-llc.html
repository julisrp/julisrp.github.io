The $25,000 Form: Form 5472 for Your LLC

Direct answer

If your US company has a foreign owner, there is an IRS form you probably owe every year: Form 5472. It applies to corporations that are 25% or more foreign-owned — including the most common case, a single-member LLC owned by a non-US person, which files it attached to a pro-forma Form 1120. The penalty for not filing is $25,000 per form, per year, and it applies even if the company sold nothing, because the trigger is not sales — it is any transaction between the company and its owner, starting with the owner putting their own money in.

Most foreign founders hear about Form 5472 the expensive way: a penalty notice for a form they never knew existed. It is not a tax form in the usual sense — for many single-member LLCs no tax is due at all — but the filing is mandatory, the deadline is real, and the fine does not scale down for small companies. Here is what the form is, who owes it, and how it gets filed.

What Form 5472 is

Form 5472 is an information return — a report, not a payment. The IRS uses it to see the money that moves between a US company and its foreign owners or related parties: contributions, loans, payments, money taken out. A US corporation that is at least 25% foreign-owned files it with its tax return. A foreign-owned single-member LLC is a disregarded entity — the IRS treats it as if it does not exist separately from its owner — but for this form it is treated as a corporation, so it files Form 5472 attached to a short, pro-forma Form 1120 (basically the name, address, and two items of the 1120, with the 5472 behind it).

The trigger almost everyone misses

The form is owed for any year in which there was a reportable transaction between the company and its foreign owner — and the bar is low. The founder wires money in to open the bank account or pay the first expenses: that is a reportable transaction. The owner takes money out, lends the company money, or pays a company bill personally: reportable. Which means the sentence founders rely on — 'my LLC had no activity' — is usually wrong in the only sense that matters here. Zero sales is not zero transactions, and zero income is not zero obligations.

The penalty: $25,000, then $25,000 more every 30 days

Fail to file — or file a form the IRS considers substantially incomplete — and the penalty is $25,000, per form and per year. If the IRS mails you a notice and you still have not filed 90 days later, another $25,000 is added for each additional 30-day period, per related party. The math is why this form has a reputation: a founder who formed an LLC in 2023 and never filed is not looking at one fine, but at one $25,000 penalty for each open year — before the continuation penalties are even counted.

When and how it is filed

The deadline is April 15, with the pro-forma Form 1120. Filing Form 7004 extends it to October 15. And here is the part that surprises everyone: a foreign-owned disregarded entity cannot e-file this return. It is sent by fax — 855-887-7737 — or by mail to the IRS service center in Ogden, Utah (M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit). No online portal, no software upload. The form also has to carry the company's EIN; if your LLC does not have one yet, the EIN is free from the IRS — nobody should charge you for the number itself.

Frequently asked questions

What is Form 5472, and who has to file it?

Form 5472 is the IRS information return for US companies with significant foreign ownership — a corporation that is 25% or more foreign-owned, including a foreign-owned single-member LLC treated as a disregarded entity. That LLC files Form 5472 attached to a pro-forma Form 1120, reporting the transactions between the company and its foreign owner.

My LLC had no sales. Do I still have to file Form 5472?

Yes, if there was any reportable transaction with the owner — and putting your own money into the company, or taking money out, already counts. Zero sales is not zero obligation: the duty to file turns on the transactions with the owner, not on revenue.

What is the penalty for not filing Form 5472?

$25,000 per form, per year. If the IRS notifies you and 90 days pass without filing, another $25,000 is added for each additional 30-day period (per related party). There is no small fine for a small company — the penalty is the same $25,000 whether the LLC earned a lot or nothing at all.

When is Form 5472 due, and how do I file it?

It is due April 15, attached to the pro-forma Form 1120, and a foreign-owned disregarded entity cannot e-file it: it goes by fax (855-887-7737) or by mail to the IRS in Ogden, Utah. Filing Form 7004 extends the deadline to October 15. A CPA prepares and files this one — it is not a do-it-yourself form.
