How Much Does an LLC Cost in the US? The 15 States Where Most Businesses Incorporate — and the 10 Most Convenient

The short answer

Forming an LLC costs between $35 and $500 in state fees, and keeping it costs between $0 and $800 a year. Those are the two numbers that matter: the price to open it and the price to keep it.

On this page you get the 15 states where most LLCs are formed in one table, plus the full detail on the 10 most convenient states to form yours.

Information verified on November 17, 2026. State fees change — sometimes with little warning — so confirm the current amount on your state's official page before you file anything.

And the honest rule, up front: form your LLC where you operate. If you form in Wyoming but your business sits in New Jersey, you pay in both states: the Wyoming LLC plus a foreign registration in New Jersey. The Top 10 below is for people who genuinely get to choose — an online business with no office or employees in a fixed state. If you physically operate in a state, that state is usually your practical pick, even when it isn't the cheapest.

The 10 most convenient states — the full detail

1. Wyoming — $100 to form, $60 a year

  • To form: $100 (Articles of Organization, filed online).
  • Each year: annual report with a license tax of $60 minimum (or 0.0002 of the value of your assets located in Wyoming, whichever is higher). Due the first day of your anniversary month.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Wyoming street address. It can be a commercial agent company; your name as owner does not appear on the Articles.
  • Steps: 1) Pick the name and check it's available. 2) Name your Wyoming registered agent. 3) File the Articles online and pay the $100. 4) Get your EIN free from the IRS. 5) Make your operating agreement (not filed with the state, but have one).
  • Speed: online filings usually approve in 1–3 business days.
  • Watch out: Wyoming has no state income tax, but that doesn't move the taxes of the state where you live or operate. And the annual report is owed even if you sell nothing.

2. Delaware — $110 to form, a flat $400 a year

  • To form: $110 (Certificate of Formation).
  • Each year: a flat $400 annual LLC tax, whether you earn anything or not. Due June 1. There is no annual report. (It rose from $300 to $400 starting with the 2026 tax year — many guides still show the old number.)
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Delaware address.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Delaware registered agent. 3) File the Certificate of Formation and pay the $110. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Internal operating agreement.
  • Speed: among the fastest; same-day service exists for an extra fee.
  • Watch out: paying the tax late costs a $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest. And Delaware's fame comes from large corporations and investors — for a small business operating in another state, you usually pay Delaware plus your state.

3. Florida — $125 to form, $138.75 a year

  • To form: $125 ($100 for the Articles + $25 for the registered agent designation).
  • Each year: an annual report of $138.75, filed between January 1 and May 1.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Florida address. You can be your own agent if you live in Florida.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability on Sunbiz. 2) Set your registered agent. 3) File the Articles online and pay the $125. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Internal operating agreement.
  • Speed: online, about 2–3 business days.
  • Watch out: a late report costs $400 extra — the total jumps to $538.75. Put the date on your calendar the day you form.

4. Texas — $300 to form, $0 tax if your revenue is up to $2.65 million

  • To form: $300 (Certificate of Formation).
  • Each year: no annual report to the Secretary of State. There is a franchise tax report plus a Public Information Report, due May 15. If your total revenue is $2.65 million or less (2026–27 period), the tax is $0 — but the report is still filed.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Texas address.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Texas registered agent. 3) File the Certificate and pay the $300. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Set up your Comptroller account for the yearly report.
  • Speed: standard processing can run up to two weeks; plan ahead.
  • Watch out: "$0 tax" does not mean "$0 paperwork." Skipping the May 15 report brings penalties even when you owe nothing.

5. Nevada — the $75 that is really $425

  • To form: the Articles cost $75, but the same day you also pay the Initial List ($150) and the State Business License ($200): $425 total to start.
  • Each year: $350 ($150 Annual List + $200 license), due the last day of your anniversary month.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Nevada address.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Nevada registered agent. 3) File Articles + Initial List + License through the state portal and pay the $425. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Check whether your city or county wants a separate local license.
  • Speed: online filings usually resolve in about 1 business day.
  • Watch out: Nevada has no state income tax, and that gets marketed hard. But at $350 a year in fixed state cost, it is one of the expensive states to keep. The Commerce Tax only applies above $4 million of Nevada revenue.

6. New Mexico — $50 to form, $0 forever

  • To form: $50 (Articles of Organization, online only).
  • Each year: nothing. New Mexico requires no annual report and charges LLCs no annual fee.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical New Mexico address and a signed statement of acceptance.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) New Mexico registered agent. 3) File the Articles in the state portal and pay the $50. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Internal operating agreement.
  • Speed: online, 1–3 business days.
  • Watch out: owner names don't appear on the Articles — it's one of the most private states. But if you sell or work inside New Mexico, the state gross receipts tax still applies. Cheap to keep does not mean invisible to taxes.

7. Arizona — $50 to form, $0 a year, with one condition

  • To form: $50 (Articles of Organization with the Arizona Corporation Commission — here it is not the Secretary of State).
  • Each year: nothing. No annual report.
  • Registered agent: called a statutory agent; required, with a physical Arizona address.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Statutory agent in Arizona. 3) File the Articles and pay the $50. 4) Handle publication if it applies to you (below). 5) EIN free from the IRS.
  • Speed: standard processing runs about two weeks; expedited (+$35) brings it down to a few days.
  • Watch out — publication: within 60 days of formation, a notice of your LLC must run 3 times in a newspaper in your agent's county, costing roughly $30–$300 depending on the paper. Exception: if your agent's address is in Maricopa County (Phoenix) or Pima County (Tucson), you publish nothing — the Commission posts it to its database for free. Most commercial agents sit in those two counties.

8. South Dakota — $150 to form, $55 a year

  • To form: $150 online ($165 on paper).
  • Each year: an annual report of $55 online ($70 on paper), due the first day of your anniversary month. Starting in 2027 you can elect to move your deadline to January 31 instead.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical South Dakota street address (no P.O. boxes).
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) South Dakota registered agent. 3) File the Articles online and pay the $150. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Internal operating agreement.
  • Speed: online, approval in about 1 business day.
  • Watch out: no state income tax and fast processing — that's why it's on this list. Paper costs $15 more on everything; file online.

9. Colorado — $50 to form, $25 a year

  • To form: $50 (Articles of Organization, online only).
  • Each year: a $25 Periodic Report. It is due in your periodic report month, and you can file from 2 months before to 2 months after with no penalty.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Colorado address. You can be your own if you live there.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Colorado registered agent. 3) File the Articles online and pay the $50. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Note your periodic report month.
  • Speed: online filings process in real time — among the fastest in the country.
  • Watch out: the report fee rose from $10 to $25 in July 2024; older guides still say $10. Forget it and the penalty is $50, and your LLC falls into noncompliant status.

10. Kentucky — $40 to form, $15 a year

  • To form: $40 (Articles of Organization) — the lowest formation fee in the country alongside Montana ($35).
  • Each year: an annual report of $15, due June 30.
  • Registered agent: required, with a physical Kentucky address.
  • Steps: 1) Name and availability. 2) Kentucky registered agent. 3) File the Articles and pay the $40. 4) EIN free from the IRS. 5) Internal operating agreement.
  • Speed: usually 1–3 business days.
  • Watch out: genuinely cheap, with no tricks hidden in the state fee. As everywhere: if you operate somewhere else, that state will want its registration too.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest state to have an LLC in?

To form one, Montana ($35) and Kentucky ($40). To keep one, Arizona, New Mexico, and Missouri: $50 to form and $0 a year, because they require no annual report. The full math is formation + years of upkeep + your registered agent.

And the most expensive?

Massachusetts: $500 to form and $500 every year. On yearly cost, California competes: its formation fee is only $70, but it charges $800 a year in franchise tax even if you sell nothing.

Can I form in Wyoming and operate in New Jersey?

You can, but it saves you nothing: New Jersey requires you to register that LLC as a foreign business ($100) and pay its annual report ($75), on top of Wyoming's $100 + $60. Two states, two payments, two reports. That's the rule: form where you operate.

How much does a registered agent cost?

If you live in the state and can receive documents during business hours, you can be your own agent and it costs $0. A commercial registered agent service typically runs $35–$300 a year, depending on the state and what's included.

Does the EIN cost anything?

No. The EIN is free, always, directly from the IRS. If someone wants to charge you to "get your EIN," they're charging you to fill out a free form.
