Voice-Ready: What It Actually Means (and Why Nobody Can Register You)

Direct answer

No — because there is nothing to register for. No voice assistant keeps a registry of businesses: Siri reads Apple Maps, Alexa reads Bing, and Google Assistant reads your Google Business Profile. If your business is hard to find by voice, the cause is ordinary — a half-finished Google profile, an Apple listing nobody claimed, your phone number written differently across the internet — and the fix is claiming those listings yourself, directly, for free. 'Voice-ready' is not an activation you buy. It is six ordinary things, done properly, and this article walks through all six.

The call usually comes in the morning, while you are working. The voice is friendly and fast: customers are asking Siri, Alexa and Google for businesses like yours — but your business is not registered for voice search, so the assistants skip it, and you are invisible to half your potential customers. The good news, the caller says, is that they can register you today. Before you pay anyone for that, read this: what the pitch gets right, what it invents, and what being findable by voice actually takes.

1. The pitch: 'you're not registered, so you're invisible'

Strip the pitch down and it has three parts. One: people really are asking voice assistants for local businesses — that part is true. Two: your business has to be 'registered' or 'activated' to appear — that part is invented. Three: the fix is a payment — sometimes a one-time fee, sometimes a monthly charge — for a registration that does not exist. The urgency is the tell: a real listing does not expire if you think about it overnight, and no assistant's database has a door that only one caller can open for you.

2. The truth: there is no registry — there are listings

Voice assistants do not keep a master list that businesses join. Each one reads business data that already exists, from its own source. Siri answers from Apple Maps, through Apple Business Connect. Alexa answers from Bing Places and the large business-data providers. Google Assistant answers from Google Business Profile — the same profile that puts you on Google Maps. Being 'in voice search' means those listings exist, are claimed by you, and agree with each other. All three are claimed directly with the platform, and claiming them costs nothing.

3. The honest numbers: big enough to matter, smaller than the pitch

The pitch usually opens with a statistic — 'over half of local searches are voice now.' Treat it as a sales line: it traces back to an old prediction that has been repeated for years as if it were a measurement. The measured picture is more modest and still worth your attention. Roughly 20% of all searches are spoken rather than typed, and about 27% of mobile searches are voice. Where voice genuinely dominates is local intent: around 76% of voice searches are local — 'near me' questions — and about 58% of consumers have used voice search to find information about a local business. So yes, customers ask assistants for businesses like yours. No, you are not invisible to half of them tonight. The direction is real; the panic is manufactured.

4. What actually makes a business invisible

When an assistant cannot find a business — or reads out the wrong phone number — the cause is almost never a missing registration. It is one of three ordinary problems. A Google Business Profile that was started and never finished: no categories, no hours, no services. An Apple listing that nobody ever claimed, still holding whatever data a directory guessed years ago. And inconsistency: your business name spelled two ways, an old address on one site, a phone number that changed in 2023 and never got updated everywhere. Assistants trust data that agrees with itself; when your listings argue with each other, the assistant stays quiet — or worse, sends the customer to the version that is wrong.

5. The voice-ready checklist: six things, in order

This is the whole job. Six things, in this order, each one free at the source:

Google Business Profile — claimed, verified, complete. Categories, hours, services, photos, the right phone number. This is what Google Assistant reads, and what Google's local results stand on.

Apple Business Connect — claimed and completed. Name, address, hours, categories. This is what Siri and Apple Maps read.

Bing Places — claimed and synced. This is what Alexa reads, along with the major data providers.

One version of your data, everywhere. Name, address and phone identical on your website, your listings and your socials. Consistency is what assistants trust — and its absence is the real cause of 'invisible.'

Reviews that are present and recent. Assistants surface businesses people vouch for. A steady flow of honest reviews does more than any 'activation.'

Answers written the way people talk. Your site should answer questions in the words customers say out loud — 'how much does it cost to…', 'who fixes… near me' — in plain sentences an assistant can read back word for word.

None of the six needs a middleman, and none of them is a registration. If someone offers to do this work for you as a service, that can be legitimate help — your time has a price too. What is never legitimate is the claim that the assistants themselves charge a door fee, or that a stranger's 'activation' is the only way in.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to register my business for voice search?

No. There is no voice-search registry — not for Siri, not for Alexa, not for Google Assistant. The assistants read business listings that already exist: Apple Maps, Bing, and your Google Business Profile. You claim and complete those listings yourself, directly with each platform, and it costs nothing. Anyone charging you to 'register' or 'activate' your business for voice search is charging for something that does not exist.

Where do voice assistants get information about my business?

Each one reads a different source. Siri answers from Apple Maps, through Apple Business Connect. Alexa answers from Bing Places and large business-data providers. Google Assistant answers from your Google Business Profile. That is why the same business can show up on one assistant and be missing on another: each listing has to be claimed and completed separately.

How much of search is really voice?

Less than the sales pitch says, and still enough to matter. Measured figures put voice at roughly 20% of all searches and about 27% of mobile searches — not the 'half of all local searches' line that circulates in ads, which traces back to an old prediction repeated as if it were a measurement. What is solid: about 76% of voice searches are local, and around 58% of consumers have used voice to look up a local business.

What actually makes a business invisible to voice assistants?

Three ordinary things: a Google Business Profile that was never completed, an Apple listing nobody claimed, and your name, address, or phone number written differently across the internet. Assistants trust data that agrees with itself. Fix those three — claimed, complete, consistent — and your business can be found and read out loud correctly.
